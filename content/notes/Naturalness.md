Let $\Lambda$ denote the energy scale associated with the onset of Beyond the Standard Model (BSM) effects, i.e., the emergence of new heavy particles or new interactions. Let us consider a scenario where there is a huge difference between $\Lambda$ and $v$. The Lagrangian takes the general form

$$\mathcal{L}=\text{const }+\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{L}_{2}+\Lambda\mathcal{L}_{3}+\mathcal{L}_{4}+\dots$$.

Here, $\mathcal{L}_d$ are field operators of mass dimension $d$. The Lagrangian consists of at least the following terms:

- $\mathcal{L}_2$ comprises the Higgs mass term $\mu^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi$
- $\mathcal{L}_3$ comprises the Dirac fermion mass terms like $y_{d}d_{R}\Phi ^{\dagger}Q_{L}$, which appear in the SM only after EW SSB
- $\mathcal{L}_{4}$ comprises all dimension-four gauge and Higgs interactions.

These terms describe the renormalisable part. The baseline power for the BSM scale $\Lambda$ is fixed by the mass dimension of the operator, since mass dimension of $\mathcal{L}$ is chosen to be four by construction. The Dirac fermion mass terms emerge only due to the breaking of the electroweak symmetry, associated with the scale $v$. Thus, the baseline power for $\mathcal{L}_3$ actually reads $\mathcal{L}\supset v\mathcal{L}_{3}$.

The so-called Hierarchy problem now arises from the fact that there is no symmetry to change the baseline power multiplying the scalar mass term $\mathcal{L}_2$ to something other than $\Lambda^2$. Since this term appears in the Higgs potential, it should also fix $v$, and hence the masses of the Dirac fermions, and the W and Z bosons.

One of the _[[raison d’être]]_ of particle physics is the unnatural size of the Higgs vev (in the absence of BSM), found up to scales of 2 TeV.
