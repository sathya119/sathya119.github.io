We will now canonically quantise the theory in the light-cone gauge. . The Poisson brackets and therefore commutators of position, momentum and transverse oscillators are the same as before

$$[x^-,p^+]=-i\;\;\;\;\;\;[x^i,p^j]=i\delta^{ij}\;\;\;\;\;\;[\alpha^i_{m},\alpha^j_{n}]=m\delta_{m+n}\delta^{i,j}$$

with all other commutators vanishing.

The composite operator $\alpha^-_{0}$ involves an ordering ambiguity since $[\alpha^i_{-n},\alpha^i_{n}]\neq 0$. We deal with this by using normal ordering

$$\alpha^-_{m}=\frac{1}{\ell p^+}\left( \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty :\alpha^i_{m-n}\alpha^i_{n}:-a\delta_{n,0} \right)$$

and parametrising our ignorance by a real parameter $a$. In particular,

$$\alpha^-_{0}=\frac{1}{\ell p^+}\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^i_{0}\alpha_{0}^i+ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \alpha^i_{-n}\alpha^i_{n}-a \right)=\frac{1}{\ell p^+}\left( \frac{1}{2}\ell^{2}p^ip^i+N-a \right)$$

and the mass-shell condition $\alpha^-=\ell p^-$ becomes 

$$\alpha'M^{2}=N-a$$.

An analogous formula holds for the closed string.

Under what circumstances does the light-cone quantisation produce a Poincaré invariant theory?

The gauge fixing $\tau=X^+/p^++\text{constant}$ explicitly breaks the symmetry group $SO(1,D-1)$ of Lorentz transformations $X^\mu\to\Lambda^\mu_{\nu}X^\nu$ to the subgroup $SO(D-2)$ of rotations $X^i\to \Lambda^i_{j}X^j$. The excitations generated by $\alpha_{n}^i$ will transform in a representation of $SO(D-2)$ but in a D-dimensional Lorentz invariant theory, they must lift to representations of $SO(1,D-1)$.

The states $\alpha_{-1}^i\ket{0;k}$ transform in the vector representation of $SO(D-2)$ with $\alpha'M=1-a$. Now, recall that a transversely polarised vector particle will acquire a longitudinal component after a general Lorentz transformation, unless it is massless. Thus, in a D-dimensional Poincaré invariant theory, the spin of a massive particle is a representation of $SO(D-1)$ while that of a massless particle is $SO(D-2)$. Therefore, light-cone gauge cannot give a Poincaré invariant theory unless $a=1$.

Now, $$\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty \alpha^i_{-n}\alpha^i_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty :\alpha^i_{-n}\alpha^i_{n}:-a$$.

This gives $$a=-\frac{D-2}{2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty n$$ from the relation $[\alpha^i_{n},\alpha^i_{-n}]=n$ for each $i=2,\dots,D-2$ and $n=1,\dots,\infty$.

Since the sum is divergent, we need to regularise it. We will use the zeta-function regularisation, which is common in QFT. This starts from the more general infinite sum 

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s}$$

which converges to the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ for $\text{Re}(s)>1$. This has an analytic continuation in the complex $s$-plane with $\zeta(-1)=-\frac{1}{12}$. Then, the regularised normal ordering constant is 

$$a=\frac{D-2}{24}$$.

If $a\stackrel{!}{=}1$, then $D\stackrel{!}{=} 26$. This is the critical dimension of the bosonic theory.
