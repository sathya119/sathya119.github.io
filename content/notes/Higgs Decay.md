Let us consider the decay modes of an [[on-shell]] Higgs boson. The properties of a particle’s decay are captured by the differential decay width $d\Gamma$. For a decay of one particle with momentum P into n particles,

$$d\Gamma= \frac{(2\pi)^4}{2M}|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\;d\Phi_{n}(P;p_{1},\dots,p_{n})$$.

$\mathcal{M}$ is the off-diagonal S-matrix element for the decay process,

$$i(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(P-p_{1}-\dots-p_{n})\mathcal{M}(P;p_{1},\dots,p_{n})=\bra{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}}S-1 \ket{P}$$,

and $d\Phi_n$ is the phase space element for the decay of a particle with mass M into n particles with momenta $p_{1},\dots,p_{n}$ and masses $m_{1},\dots,m_{n}$.

$$d\Phi_{n}(P;p_{1},\dots,p_{n})=\delta^{(4)}\left( P-\sum_{i=1}^n p_{i} \right)\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{d^{3}p_{i}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{i}}$$.

Since we require only the $1\rightarrow 2$,

$$d\Phi_{2}(P;p_{1},p_{2})=\frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{(2\pi)^6} \frac{|\vec{p}_{1}|}{2M}d\Omega,\;\;\;\;\;\;|\vec{p}_{1}|= \frac{\lambda^{1/2}(M^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})}{2M}$$.

Here, $\lambda(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-2xy-2xz-2yz$ is the Källén function and $d\Omega=d\cos \theta d\phi$ is the solid angle element of the first decay product in the center-of-mass frame of the initial particle. Since the Higgs boson is a scalar particle, the decay width is isotropic, i.e., the matrix element does not depend on the solid angle. Hence, 

$$\Gamma= \frac{1}{16\pi M^{3}}|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\lambda^{1/2}(M^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})$$.
