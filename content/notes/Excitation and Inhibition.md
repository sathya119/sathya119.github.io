# Electrical Terminology

- **Conductor** - Object or substance that allows the flow of electric current.
- **Current (I)** - The flow of charge through a conductor per unit time.
- **Resistance** - A measure of how much a conductor opposes the flow of current; unit: $\Omega$ ohms.
- **Resistor** - An electrical component with a calibrated resistance.
- **Conductance** - A measure of how easily electric current can flow through a conductor. unit $\mho$ mho or Siemens.
- **Capacitance** - Ability to store charge.
- **Capacitor** - An electrical component that can store charge, with a calibrated capacitance.

# Cellular Terminology

- **Cytoplasm** - Everything within a cell except the nucleus.
- **Axon** - A nerve fibre projecting away from the cell body that conducts electrical impulses (action potentials).
- **Axial Resistance** - The overall resistance to flow of ions down a neuron’s axon.
- **Membrane Resistance** - The overall resistance to flow of ions across a membrane.
- **Myelination** - Process in which the membranes of the axon are wrapped in a fatty tissue called _myelin_.

# Measurements, Values, Equations

- In neurons, resistance is typically measured in $\Omega/mm^{2}$ with respect to patches of membrane.
- **Ohm’s Law** - The current (I) flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage (V) across it, under constant temperature and a given material. The constant of proportionality is resistance (R); $V=IR$.
- **Length Constant** - $\lambda$ is the distance it takes for a change to result in the membrane potential falling to 37% of its original value while traveling. The membrane resistance is represented in $\Omega\;cm$ and the axial resistance is represented in $\Omega/cm$.
	- The membrane resistance is proportional to the length constant. The axial resistance is inversely proportional to the length constant.
	- When given the voltage of one neuron (a), the voltage of another (b) and the distance between them (c), we can find the length constant:
		1. Solve for $x$: $a(0.37^x)=b$.
		2. Find $c/x$.
- **Time Constant** - Describes how long it takes a piece of membrane to charge up to 63% of its final value in response to a change in its input voltage.

# Introduction to Resistance, Capacitance, Current and Ohm’s Law

Many factors influence the resistance of a neuron and its components. For instance, ion channels have their own resistance; larger channels have more space for ions to flow through, resulting in a lower resistance. Likewise, a larger number of ion channels will effectively reduce the membrane’s resistance. The greater the width of the axon, lesser is its resistance.

The cell membrane acts like a capacitor, in which charges build up along the intracellular and extracellular sides, resulting in a gradual change in voltage (membrane potential) as opposed to and instantaneous one. However, rather than saying that the entire membrane acts as a single capacitor, it is more correct to say there are multiple capacitors working in parallel along the cell membrane. Channels act as resistors, allowing the current of ions to leak from the cell as their conductance and permeability are altered.

# Resistance in the Neuron

In a cell, mobile charges are carried by ions in the intracellular and extracellular solutions. These ionic currents principally flow in two directions, across the membrane and down the cytoplasm. So while observing the current in a cell, we need to consider the axial and membrane resistances. This is where the length constant comes into play.

# Membrane Capacitance

Changing the capacitance of a membrane comes into play only when the voltage changes over time. Decreasing (e.g., thicker membrane) or increasing (e.g., thinner membrane) the capacitance makes the membrane more responsive or sluggish to change respectively. Depending on the situation, sending a signal quickly or over time may be advantageous.
