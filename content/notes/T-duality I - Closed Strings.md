So far, we have considered strings propagating on a flat space-time $\mathbb{R}^{1,D-1}$. However, consistency demands dimension $D>4$. The simplest example is to modify the geometry to $\mathbb{R}^{1,D-2}\times S^1$, where $S^1$ is a circle of radius $R$. This leads us to a surprising phenomenon called T-duality, which indicates that strings probe geometry in a fundamentally different way to particles. 

First, some notation. We define $d:=D-1$ and the coordinates $x^\mu=\{x^0,x^1,\dots,x^d\}$. We also require that the final coordinate is periodic, i.e., $x^d\sim x^d+2\pi R$. It is then convenient to index the remaining non-periodic coordinates by $x^m=\{x^0,x^1,\dots,x^{d-1}\}$.
