Nature’s camera.

Cornea, Lens - Focusses the light.
Ciliary Muscles - Changes the shape of lens to focus the light.
Retina - Light is recorded by photoreceptors (~100 million), that convert light energy into neuronal activity. Also does image processing by sending the signal to the optic nerve.
Eyeball - Able to pivot around with the help of Oculomotor Muscles.
Pupil - Controls illumination entering the eye.
Tears and Blinking - Cleans the eye.

The photoreceptors are distributed very unevenly across the retina. Most dense at the center of your gaze in a region called Fovea. This gives a high resolution image distance of only about the width of your thumb away by the length of your arm.

>You can try to focus on a cross or a point and see for yourself that in a while, you will realise how blurry your vision actually is!

We think we see the world in a highly crisp resolution only because the eye moves about 3 times per second.

The retina is like a 1 MP camera since only about a million axons carry information away from the retina. But, the eye moves around a lot and samples the scene.
