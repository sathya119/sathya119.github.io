- Theoretical considerations have provided and continue to provide powerful constraints on Higgs physics, both in the Standard Model and beyond.
- The Nambu-Goldstone theorem predicts the existence of massless scalar particles in the presence of spontaneously broken symmetries.
- The equivalence theorem connects the scattering amplitudes of highly-boosted massive gauge bosons with those of the Nambu-Goldstone bosons. • The unitarity constraint provides an upper bound on the Higgs mass $m_h$ (or equivalently the self coupling $\lambda$), which was known to be within the reach of the LHC before the discovery of the Higgs boson.
- The electroweak precision observables provide an indirect determination of the Higgs mass, which was a powerful constraint on the Higgs sector of the Standard Model even prior to the discovery of the Higgs boson at the LHC.
- The degree of fine-tuning in the Higgs sector is used to set (subjective) upper bounds on the scale $\Lambda$ at which beyond-the-Standard Model physics is expected to emerge.
