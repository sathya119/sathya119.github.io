Shruti is simply the reference pitch for you to base your singing on. It is like the foundation of a building, which supports anything else that is built on top of it later on.

To start with, learn about [[Notations - Shruti]]

Now, a very common problem that beginners face, is to decide what Shruti to sing in.

*A widely accepted rule of thumb is for male singers, the Shruti is C/D (Western notation) or 1P/2P (Indian notation) and for female singers it is G/A (Western) or 5P/6P (Indian).
Although, this is usually not the case. You will find male singers singing in Shrutis as low as A (6P) or as high as E (3P) and female singers singing as low as F (3P) or as high as C (1P).*

Another trouble that most beginners face is ensuring that they stick to the Shruti they are keeping as a reference. To put it in a different way, how does one know if they are on pitch or not?

*First, sing Sa in that Shruti and check if your pitch is matching with that of the Tanpura. Next, sing Pa and check for the same. Finally, sing the higher Sa and check for a match. If all the 3 swaras match, congratulations! You passed the first test.*
Then, if one sings Sa-Pa-Sa in pitch, is that enough to stick to a Shruti? Well no, this is just to begin with. Once you have mastered this, you can try the same with other notes by keeping Sa as your reference.
