We can supplement open strings with Neumann boundary conditions by Chan-Paton degrees of freedom. Since the end-points of an open string act like particles, we can add additional internal degrees of freedom localised at the endpoints, which are separate from the string dynamics.

Suppose each end-point can in $N$ linearly independent states $\ket{j}$ indexed by $j=1,\dots,N$. Then, open string states are obtained by acting with raising operators on ground states 

$$\ket{0;k;i,j} $$

corresponding to strings with state $\ket{i}$ at $\sigma=0$ and $\ket{j}$ at $\sigma=\pi$. These strings will be called as $ij$-strings. The additional discrete labels are known as Chan-Paton factors.

We now introduce a complete set of ground states,

$$\ket{0;k;a}=\sum_{i,j=1}^N \lambda_{ij}^a \ket{0;k;i,j} $$

where $\lambda^a_{ij}$ denotes a basis of Hermitian matrices indexed by $a=1,\dots,N^{2}$ and normalised such that $\text{Tr}(\lambda^a\lambda^b)=\delta^{ab}$. Inner products are then invariant under transformations $\lambda^a\mapsto U\lambda^aU^{\dagger}$, where $U$ is an $N\times N$ unitary matrix. Thus, open strings states now transform in the adjoint representation of $U(N)$.

When $a=1$, we have massless states $$\lambda^a_{ij}\alpha_{-1}^\mu \ket{0;k;i,j} $$ which correspond to excitations of a non-abelian $U(N)$ gauge field $A_{\mu}=A^a_{\mu}\lambda^a$. In this way, open strings with Chan-Paton factors naturally produce $U(N)$ gauge fields with end-points of the string behaving as point particles transforming in the fundamental and anti-fundamental representations.

All strings states are neutral under the diagonal $U(1)\subset U(N)$ corresponding to the generator $\lambda^a_{ij}=\delta_{ij}/\sqrt{ N }$. Equivalently, the adjoint representation of $U(1)$ is trivial and these gauge fields decouple from the spectrum.
