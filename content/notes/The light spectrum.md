#### Open String

The mass-shell condition is $\alpha'M^{2}=N-1$ and thus, the spectrum can be summarised as:

- Ground state $\ket{0;k}$ has $\alpha'M^{2}=-1$. It is a tachyonic scalar.
- Level-one states $\varepsilon_{i}\alpha^i_{-1}\ket{0;k}$ have $\alpha'M^{2}=0$. They transform in a vector representation $\mathbf{24}$ of $SO(24)$ and correspond to the transverse physical polarisations of a massless vector field $A_{\mu}$.
- States at higher levels are massive with $\alpha'M^{2}>0$. A [[priori]], they transform in tensor representations of $SO(24)$. However, since the states correspond to polarisations of massive tensor fields, they must assemble into representations of $SO(25)$.

#### Closed Strings

The mass-shell and level-matching conditions are

$$\alpha'M^{2}=2(N+\tilde{N}-2)\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;N-\tilde{N}=0$$

such that $\alpha'M^{2}=4(N-1)$. We summarise the spectrum as follows:

- Ground state $\ket{0;k}$ has $\alpha'M^{2}=-4$ and corresponds to a scalar tachyon.
- Level-one states $\varepsilon_{ij}\alpha^i_{-1}\tilde{\alpha}^j_{-1}\ket{0;k}$ have $\alpha'M^{2}=0$. They transform in the $\mathbf{24}\otimes\mathbf{24}$ representation of $SO(24)$, which decomposes as a symmetric traceless tensor $\mathbf{299}$, anti-symmetric tensor $\mathbf{276}$ and singlet $\mathbf{1}$. They correspond to the transverse polarisations of the graviton $g_{\mu \nu}$, Kalb-Ramond field $B_{\mu \nu}$ and dilaton $\Phi$.
- States at higher levels are massive with $\alpha'M^{2}>0$. A priori, they transform in tensor representations of $SO(24)$. However, since the states correspond to polarisations of massive tensor fields, they must assemble into representations of $SO(25)$.

We emphasise again, the remarkable fact that the fluctuation of closed strings necessarily include excitations behaving as gravitons and this is the reason for interest in string theory as a theory of quantum gravity.
