Anatomically, it has at least 10 distance layers, which can be simplified into three main functional stages and two synaptic layers:

1. Photoreception
2. Internal Transmission
3. Output (into the deeper brain)

These are arranged in the opposite order though (Photoreceptors being in the deeper layers and the output layer being at front).

Once detected by the photoreceptors, the signal is transmitted synaptically and processed by a series of interneurons (including bipolar cells), and further passed on synaptically to the retinal ganglion cells which act as feature detectors.

At each synaptic stage, there are laterally connecting cells called the horizontal and amacrine cells that allow synchronisation and some integration of the signal input.

Axons of the retinal ganglion cells are then bundled into the optic nerve, which distributes visual information in the form of parallel action potentials. These features are then sent on to several deep brain systems.
