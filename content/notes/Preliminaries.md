#### DM Flux

The typical velocity of DM particles is of the order of $\sim 10^3 \;c$. The local DM density is $\rho_{0}= 0.3\text{ GeV cm}^{-3}$ and thus, the DM number density is $n=\rho/m$.

$$\phi=\frac{v\rho}{m}\approx \frac{10^7}{m}\text{ cm}^{-2}\text{s}^{-1}$$.

Assuming a typical WIMP cross section $\sigma$, these particles interact very weakly with SM particles.

#### Kinematics

Direct DM detection is based on the search of scattering between DM particles and nuclei in a detector. This process is observable only through the recoiling nucleus, with an energy $E_R$. Since DM particles move at non-relativistic speeds, the dynamics of their elastic scattering off nuclei can be calculated.

$$E_{R}= \frac{1}{2}m_{\chi}v^{2} \frac{4m_{\chi}m_{N}}{(m_{\chi}+m_{N})^{2}} \frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}$$.

For DM particles with a mass of the order of $100\text{ GeV}$, this leads to recoil energies of approximately $E_{R}\sim 100\text{ keV}$. In the case of a head-on collision and when the DM and target masses are equal,

$$E_{R}^{\text{max}}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\chi}v^{2}=\frac{1}{2}m_{chi}\times 10^{-6}=\frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{m_{\chi}}{1\text{ GeV}} \right)\text{ keV}$$.
