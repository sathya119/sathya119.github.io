# Terminology

- **Synaptic Integration** - Process by which neurons combine information from many synaptic transmissions at once.
- **The Neuron Doctrine** - Description of the main properties of neurons and neuronal systems with regard to neurotransmission, including the existence of neurons as discrete cells, the reception of input signals through their dendrites, the integration of those signals into their soma, and their determination of whether or not to send an output signal through their axon.
- **Circuit** - A group of interconnected neurons, mostly with specialised functions.
- **Dendritic Shaft** - Main cylindrical trunk of the dendrite.
- **Dendritic Spines** - Tiny protrusions from the dendrite that specialise in receiving excitatory inputs.
- **Axon Initial Segment** - Base of the axon that is connected to the soma; aka _Axon Hillock_.
- **Nonlinear Summation** - A combined amplitude of the excitatory signals; not exactly the mathematical sum of the individual amplitudes.
- **Synaptic Summation** - The nonlinear integration of the simultaneous excitatory signals of two presynaptic cells into the soma.
- **Synaptic Inhibition** - The cancelling of a simultaneous excitatory signal by an inhibitory signal.
- **Backpropagation** - Process in which the cytoplasm first conducts the dendritic postsynaptic potentials to the soma and then conducts current from somatic action potentials back into the dendritic arbour; Current actively flows from dendrites into the soma and then passively backwashes from the soma into the dendritic arbour.
- **Synaptic Temporal Summation** - The nonlinear combined effect of a single excitatory neuron firing multiple times in a rapid succession that is fast enough for one of its EPSPs to arrive at the postsynaptic site during the falling phase of a preceding EPSP.
- **Synaptic Temporal Inhibition** - A phenomenon in which an IPSP arrives between two successive EPSPs, tamping down the effects of temporal summation that would have occurred in its absence.
- **Convergence** - Outcome of multiple (potentially disparate) inputs coming together to synapse on a single downstream neuron.
- **Divergence** - A neural mechanism by which one neuron sends a signal to many other neurons, supporting the coordination between multiple systems.
- **Monosynaptic Reflex** - A simple reflex that involves only one synapse between a sensory and motor neuron.
- **Myotatic Reflex** - A reflex resulting from a “pure” convergence and divergence in the nervous system.
