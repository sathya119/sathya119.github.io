The QCD Lagrangian presented so far has discrete global symmetries under C (charge), P (parity) and T (time reversal). The most general Lagrangian with SU(Nc) gauge symmetry contains one more term 

$$\mathcal{L}_\theta=\frac{\theta g_s^2}{32\pi^2}tr[F_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{\mu\nu}] \;\;\;\; \text{ with }\;\;\;\;\tilde F^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}$$

This term violates CP symmetry and can contribute non-perturbatively. Current experimental bounds on neutron dipole moment indicate that $$\theta < 10^{-10}$$. The fact that this term is very small (almost zero) is called the strong CP problem.
