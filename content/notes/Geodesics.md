The Geodesic equation is $$V^\mu\nabla_\mu V^\nu=0$$
We can write this in terms of #Christoffel-symbols as $$V^\mu\nabla_\mu V^\nu=V^\mu(\partial_\mu V^\nu+\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\lambda}V^\lambda)=\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\lambda}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\lambda}{d\lambda}=0$$
When we choose the metric as the #Minkowski metric, the #Christoffel-symbols vanish and hence the geodesic equation recovers straight lines. 
A consequence is that the length of the tangent vector $$V^\lambda\partial _\lambda(g_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu)=V^\lambda\nabla_\lambda(g_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu)=2V^\lambda V^\mu g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\nu=0$$
By choice of the parameter, we can set the length to be ±1,0 (Proper length (+1) or Proper time (-1)).
Alternatively, we can define geodesics as an extrema of proper length or proper time.
	This method of finding the Euler-Lagrangian equations resulting from the proper length can be an efficient way to calculate the #Christoffel-symbols in curved geometry.
Steps:
- Write the EL equation and choose the parameter as proper length. Set the length of the tangent vector to 1.
- Obtain the equations and compare with the Geodesic equation.
- Write the #Christoffel-symbols .
