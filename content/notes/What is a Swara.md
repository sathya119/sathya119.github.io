A Swara or a note is basically a fixed frequency. For example, 440 Hz (an international standard) is A4 or the note A in the 4th octave.

>[[What is so special about 440 Hz]]?

In Carnatic Music, a Swara cannot be defined by a standalone frequency but is always connected to a reference.

There are seven notes, popularly called the Sapta Swaras (Sapta: Sanskrit for seven). They are:
- Sa - Shadja
- Ri - Rishabha
- Ga - Gandhara
- Ma - Madhyama
- Pa - Panchama
- Dha - Dhaivata
- Ni - Nishada

>[[What do Swara names mean]]?

The Sapta Swaras are often notated as S R G M P D N for ease.

When a Shruti is chosen, the notes that are used as the drone are Sa and Pa (sometimes Ma). Hence, these two notes are stationary and have no variants whatsoever, i.e., their frequencies are fixed for a particular Shruti.

The rest 5 have 2 variants each.
- Ri - R1, R2
- Ga - G1, G2
- Ma - M1, M2
- Dha - D1, D2
- Ni - N1, N2

Hence, in total, we have seven Swaras and 12 Swara Sthanas (Sthana: Sanskrit for place/position). This can be visually understood by looking a piano.
