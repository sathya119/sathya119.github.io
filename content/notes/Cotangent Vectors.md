Given the tangent space Tₚ, we can define the cotangent vector space Tₚ* as the space of linear maps from Tₚ to the n-dimensional real space. These maps are called covectors, normal vectors or one-forms.
- The differential df of a function f is a covector, a normal vector to the surface f = constant.
If the coordinates are function on M, the associated differentials form a basis in Tₚ*, which is dual to the basis in Tₚ, $$dx^\nu(\partial_\mu) = \partial_\mu(x^\nu)=\delta^\nu_\mu$$Then, the if the maps $$\omega=\omega_\mu dx^\mu$$ we have $$\omega(\partial_\mu)=w_\nu dx^\nu(\partial_\mu)=w_\nu\delta^\nu_\mu=\omega_\mu$$
We can also define a cotangent vector field, a choice of covector at each point p in M. Given a general cotangent vector field there will generally not exist a function such that the cotangent vector field is its differential.
Now, under coordinate transformation, $$\omega=\omega_\nu dx^\nu=\tilde{\omega}_\mu d\tilde{x}^\mu = \tilde{\omega}_\mu\frac{\partial\tilde{x}^\mu}{\partial x^\nu}dx^\nu$$So the components transform as, $$\omega_\nu = \tilde{\omega}_\mu\frac{\partial\tilde{x}^\mu}{\partial x^\nu}$$
What about the combination of a vector and a covector? $$\tilde{\omega}_\mu\tilde{V}^\mu = \frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial \tilde{x}^\mu}\omega_\nu\frac{\partial \tilde{x}^\nu}{\partial x^\rho}V_\rho=\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial x^\rho}\omega_\nu V^\rho=\delta^\nu_\rho\omega_\nu V^\rho=w_\nu V^\nu$$We say that the indices are contracted.
