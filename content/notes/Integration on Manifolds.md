The integral of a scalar function f(x) over a region V in cartesian coordinates is 

$$\int_V d^dxf(x)$$

and under a coordinate transformation, 

$$d^dx=Jd^d\tilde{x}$$

where J is the #Jacobian 

$$J=\begin{vmatrix}\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial\tilde{x}^\nu}\end{vmatrix}$$

Similarly, for the metric, 

$$\tilde{g}=\begin{vmatrix}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\end{vmatrix}=-J^2\implies J=\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}$$

Thus, in a general coordinate system, the integral of a function f over a region V is defined as 

$$\int_V d^dx\sqrt{-g}f(x)$$

In particular, if we integrate with f(x)=1, the result is the proper volume of the region V.
