We consider a massless scalar field in $D$-dimensions satisfying the wave equation $\partial ^\mu \partial_{\mu}\phi=0$. Expanding in a complete set of periodic functions under $x^d\to x^d+2\pi R$, we find

$$\phi(x^\mu)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty \phi_{n}(x^m)e^{inx^d/R}$$.

The momentum measured by $P_{d}=i\partial_{d}$, quantised as $p_{d}=n/R$ where the integer $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ is known as the Kaluza-Klein (KK) momentum. The modes $\phi_{n}(x^m)$ therefore behave as $d$-dimensional fields with $$m^{2}:=-p^mp_{m}=\left( \frac{n}{R} \right)^{2}$$.

In the limit $R\to \infty$, the massive states become light and numerous, and the momentum tends to a continuum, reproducing the $D$-dimensional theory. In the limit $R\to0$, the modes with $n\neq 0$ become infinitely massive and decouple, leaving a $d$-dimensional theory of the constant massless mode.

We also introduce a non-trivial space-time metric $g_{\mu \nu}$. This has a decomposition into components $g_{mn}$, $A_{m}:=g_{md}$ and $\varphi:=g_{dd}$. Infinitesimal coordinate transformations $x^\mu\to x^\mu+\epsilon^\mu$ transform the metric by $$g_{\mu \nu}\to g_{\mu \nu}-\partial_{\mu}\epsilon_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\epsilon_{\mu}$$.

If we restrict ourselves to coordinate transformations of the form $x^d\to x^d+\epsilon(x^m)$, then $$A_{m}\to A_{m}-\partial_{m}\epsilon$$ while $g_{mn}$ and $\varphi$ are left invariant. Thus the component $A_{m}$ transforms as a $U(1)$ gauge field in $d$-dimensions where gauge transformations arise from coordinate transformations in higher dimensions.

Provided $\varphi=1$, we can understand from the mode expansion that the $d$-dimensional fields are charged under the $U(1)$ gauge symmetry. Basically, there is a tower of massive fields $\phi_{n}$ of mass $m^{2}=n^{2}/R^{2}$ and $U(1)$ charge $n$.
