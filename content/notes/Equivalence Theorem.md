We require the description of a massive gauge boson V that carries momentum k. It also carries a polarisation state, which we denote as the four-vector $\epsilon$. They are

$$\epsilon(k,\lambda=+1)^\mu=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(0,1,+i,0),\;\;\;\epsilon(k,\lambda=-1)^\mu=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(0,1,-i,0),\;\;\;\epsilon(k,\lambda=0)^\mu=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(0, 0, 0, 1)$$.

The polarisation states are $\lambda_{i}=\pm1,0$, corresponding to two transverse polarisations and one longitudinal polarisation.

Now, let us assume that our boson $V$ is highly-boosted, along the z-direction. This leads to a momentum $k=(E,0,0,|k|)$, where $E=\sqrt{ |k|^{2}+m^{2}_{V}}$. For a highly-relativistic gauge boson, we have $E\approx|k|\gg M_{V}$ and therefore, $k^\mu\rightarrow(|k|,0,0,|k|)$. The boost along the z-direction leaves the two transverse polarisation vectors unchanged, since they are orthogonal to the boost direction. However, for the longitudinal polarisation state, we find

$$\epsilon(k,\lambda=0)^\mu=\frac{1}{M_{V}}(|k|,0,0,E)\xrightarrow{E\gg M_{V}}\frac{1}{M_{V}}(|k|,0,0,|k|)= \frac{1}{M_{V}}k^\mu$$.

Hence, we can identify the longitudinal polarisation vector of the gauge bosons as the momentum $k$ _in the limit of highly-boosted gauge bosons_. This relation between the Nambu-Goldstone bosons and the gauge bosons at high energies is called the equivalence theorem.
